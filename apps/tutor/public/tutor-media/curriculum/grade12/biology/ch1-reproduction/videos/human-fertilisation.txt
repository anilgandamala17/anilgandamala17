Human Fertilisation Process
Grade 12 Biology · Chapter 1 Reproduction · Topic 2 Fertilization
Duration: 6 minutes 23 seconds

Transcript
-------------
Fertilisation is the fusion of haploid gametes to form a diploid zygote — the start of a new organism.

In humans, millions of sperm travel toward a single oocyte still wrapped in protective layers after ovulation.

Sperm must reach the cumulus cells and the zona pellucida, a glycoprotein coat around the egg.

Capacitation in the female tract prepares sperm membranes so they can undergo the acrosome reaction.

The acrosome is a cap-like vesicle on the sperm head. It releases enzymes that digest a path through the zona pellucida so the sperm can reach the egg plasma membrane.

Membrane fusion lets the sperm nucleus enter the egg cytoplasm. The cortical reaction then hardens the zona pellucida and blocks other sperm (block to polyspermy).

Male and female pronuclei — each haploid — come together. Their nuclear membranes break down and chromosomes align on a shared spindle for the first mitotic division.

The zygote is diploid (n + n = 2n) and totipotent. Cleavage divisions follow without much growth between mitoses, producing blastomeres that lead toward the morula and blastocyst.

Key ideas: approach, acrosome reaction, fusion, block to polyspermy, then a diploid zygote ready for embryonic development.
